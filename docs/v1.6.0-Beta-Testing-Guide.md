# v1.6.0 — Beta Testing Guide

**Type:** Documentation and product-planning exercise only. No code was
written, no branch was created, no files in the application were
modified, and no Pull Request was opened for this document. This
document — and the three others delivered alongside it
(`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Feedback-Questions.md`,
`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Release-Notes.md`, `docs/v1.6.0-Known-Limitations.md`)
— were authored directly on the already-checked-out
`v1.6.0-ux-polish-phase-1` branch and are **intentionally left
uncommitted**. The only exception to "do not modify the repository" is
the creation of these four requested files, per the explicit "Create:"
instruction. No other file was touched.

**Audience for this document:** whoever is running the beta program
(support/product, not necessarily an engineer), and it can also be handed
directly to beta testers as a facilitation script.

**Grounding:** every journey, confusion point, and duration estimate
below is drawn from two prior, directly-verified pieces of work — the
live, first-time-user walkthrough in `docs/v1.6.0-Product-Experience-Review.md`
and the before/after re-validation in `docs/v1.6.0-UX-Polish-Phase-1.md`
— not invented for this document. Where a confusion point was already
fixed in UX Polish Phase 1, it is marked **(resolved)** below rather than
silently omitted, so this guide stays an honest record of the platform as
it exists today, post-Phase-1.

---

## How to use this guide

Run each journey with a real beta tester, on a real signed-up account,
with no engineer sitting next to them prompting. Time it. Note every
place they pause, ask "what do I click," or navigate somewhere the
journey didn't ask them to go — that hesitation is the actual signal, not
whether they eventually succeed. A tester who gets there after 90 seconds
of confusion "succeeded" by the checklist below but still surfaced a real
UX problem worth recording.

## Beta User Journeys

### Journey 1 — First Login

**Steps:** land on `/login` → sign up (name/email/password) → arrive at
the Command Centre.

**Expected duration:** 30–60 seconds.

**Potential confusion points:**
- The full sidebar (10 groups, dozens of items) and a notification bell
  render on the pre-authentication login page itself — a tester may
  wonder if they're already "in" the app before signing up.
- Once inside, the Command Centre immediately shows real-looking numbers
  (a Portfolio Health Score, "3 open positions," a Risk Limit Breached
  banner) for an account that is seconds old — this is intentional seeded
  demo data (`ensureSeedTrades()`), but nothing on screen says so. Expect
  testers to ask "wait, did I already do something?"

**Success criteria:** the tester reaches the Command Centre without
needing help, and can correctly state (when asked) whether the account
data they're seeing is real or a demo starting point. If they can't
answer that second part confidently, record it as a miss even though the
navigation itself succeeded.

---

### Journey 2 — First Market Scan

**Steps:** Command Centre → Scanner (via the AI Trading Coach's own "Next
Recommended Step," the sidebar, or the journey breadcrumb) → run a scan.

**Expected duration:** 1–2 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- **(resolved in UX Polish Phase 1)** The AI Trading Coach panel used to
  occupy the entire screen on load, hiding the Scanner's own title and
  content. Confirm live that the tester sees "Market Scanner" and its
  description immediately, without scrolling.
- Scanner's own "Market Scan" workflow step can show as already complete
  (green checkmark) before the tester has clicked "Run Scan" themselves,
  if scanner results already exist for today — expect a "wait, did that
  already happen?" moment.

**Success criteria:** the tester finds and uses the "Run Scan" button
without prompting, and can explain in one sentence what the ranked list
they get back represents.

---

### Journey 3 — First Research Session

**Steps:** from a scanned opportunity or the sidebar → Trading Research
(Engine 2, technical) or Value Research (Engine 1, fundamental) → search
a real symbol.

**Expected duration:** 2–4 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- The platform has two, differently-purposed "Research" surfaces
  (Trading Research vs. Value Research/Stock Research) plus a Research
  Terminal that composes both — a tester coming from Scanner (Engine 3,
  options income) may not immediately know which one to open, since none
  of the three is explicitly cross-linked from the Scanner page itself.
- **(resolved in UX Polish Phase 1)** The sidebar footer used to show
  "Sign in" on Trading Research specifically, even for an authenticated
  user, right as this heavier page loaded many concurrent requests —
  confirm live this no longer happens on a normal page visit.

**Success criteria:** the tester lands on a real symbol's research view
and can identify at least one concrete signal (e.g., trend, liquidity, a
valuation verdict) the page is showing them, in their own words.

---

### Journey 4 — First Trade Plan

**Steps:** from Decision Workflow's own prompt ("Open the AI Assistant to
create a Trade Plan") **or** from Trade Planning & Risk Studio directly →
create a plan.

**Expected duration:** 3–5 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- **The platform genuinely has two, non-interoperating "Trade Plan"
  systems** (an AI-Assistant-created one, and a separate symbol-specific
  one in Trade Planning & Risk Studio). UX Polish Phase 1 added
  disambiguation notes explaining this on both surfaces, but this is the
  single highest-risk journey in the whole beta — watch closely for
  whether a tester who creates a plan one way later looks for it in the
  other place and can't find it, even with the new notes present.
- The AI Assistant's own chat-based creation flow is a different
  interaction pattern (conversational) than Trade Planning & Risk
  Studio's own form-based one (fill in entry/stop/target) — a tester may
  expect both to work the same way.

**Success criteria:** the tester creates one real plan, states out loud
which of the two systems they used, and can correctly predict which
other page(s) will and won't show that same plan. This last part is the
real test of whether the disambiguation notes work — do not skip it.

---

### Journey 5 — First Decision Review

**Steps:** Decision Workflow → select the trade plan created in Journey 4
→ read the Decision score/evidence.

**Expected duration:** 2–3 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- Decision Workflow is a distinct page from the separate, single-symbol
  "Decision Engine" — both exist, both are named similarly, and a tester
  may open the wrong one first.
- The Decision score is explicitly "guidance only, never a buy/sell
  instruction" — watch for whether a tester reads it as a recommendation
  anyway; if they do, that's a genuine wording/framing risk worth
  recording, not just a navigation one.

**Success criteria:** the tester can state, unprompted, that the score is
informational rather than an instruction, and can name at least one
input (Trade Plan, Strategy, Journal, or Portfolio) the score is drawn
from.

---

### Journey 6 — First Execution

**Steps:** from Decision Workflow's own journey breadcrumb ("Next: Open
Position") → either Trade Execution Center (submit a real Paper Trading
order) **or** Execution & Lifecycle Manager (track a trade already
executed manually elsewhere).

**Expected duration:** 3–5 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- **(largely resolved in UX Polish Phase 1)** These two pages both used
  to lead with the word "Execution" with no stated difference between
  them; each page's header now states its own job in one sentence with a
  link to the other. Confirm live that a tester reads this note (don't
  just confirm it renders — confirm it's actually read and understood)
  before assuming this confusion is fully gone.
- Trade Execution Center's "Execution" step can show a `[Blocked]` badge
  when markets are closed — confirm the tester can now see *why* inline
  (this was previously unexplained; UX Polish Phase 1 added the reason
  text directly under the badge).

**Success criteria:** the tester correctly picks the page matching their
actual intent (submit a real Paper order vs. track a manual one) on the
first try, without opening both to compare.

---

### Journey 7 — First Journal Entry

**Steps:** Trading Journal (Engine 2) or the options-side Journal → write
a reflection on a trade.

**Expected duration:** 1–2 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- **(resolved in UX Polish Phase 1)** Trading Journal used to be the
  single worst page in the whole platform for this exact problem — the
  AI Trading Coach panel used to occupy 100% of the visible screen, with
  zero journal content visible without scrolling. Confirm live this is
  now fixed: the page title and the "New Entry" form should both be
  visible on load.
- The platform has three "Journal"-named surfaces (the original options
  Journal, Trading Journal, and AI Trade Journal) — a tester may open the
  wrong one and wonder where their entry went.

**Success criteria:** the tester writes and saves one real entry without
needing to be told which of the three Journal pages to use.

---

### Journey 8 — First Portfolio Review

**Steps:** Portfolio Dashboard (risk/health rollup) or Portfolio AI
(Ravish cockpit) → review the account's current state.

**Expected duration:** 1–2 minutes.

**Potential confusion points:**
- This is one of the platform's already-strongest pages (called out as
  "already excellent" in the original review) — expect this journey to
  go smoothly; if a tester still struggles here, treat it as a stronger
  signal than a struggle on a weaker page, since the bar for this page is
  already high.
- A brand-new account's own seeded-demo Portfolio Health Score reading
  "Caution" (from Journey 1's own seeded data) can read as alarming with
  no first-time framing.

**Success criteria:** the tester can state their account's overall
health/risk read in their own words within 60 seconds of landing on the
page.

## Beta Checklist

Give this directly to testers as a self-report form, filled in
immediately after their own free-form walkthrough (not while reading this
guide) — the goal is to capture what they actually remember and
understood, not what they can find by re-reading instructions.

**Discovery**
- [ ] Could you find the Scanner without help?
- [ ] Did you know where to research a stock or option before trading it?
- [ ] Could you find your way back to the Command Centre from any page?
- [ ] Did you ever have to use the sidebar search (⌘K/Ctrl+K) because you
      couldn't find something another way?

**The AI Trading Coach**
- [ ] Did you understand what the AI Trading Coach was for the first time
      you saw it?
- [ ] Did the coach panel ever feel like it was in your way?
- [ ] Did you know how to see the coach's full step-by-step checklist?
- [ ] Did the coach's "Next Recommended Step" ever feel wrong or
      unhelpful for what you were actually trying to do?

**Trade Planning & Decision Review**
- [ ] Did you understand Trade Planning — i.e., what a "Trade Plan"
      actually is on this platform?
- [ ] If you created more than one Trade Plan, did they all show up in
      the same place?
- [ ] Did you understand the difference between Decision Review and
      Execution?
- [ ] Did you understand that the Decision score is guidance, not an
      instruction to buy or sell?

**Execution**
- [ ] Did you understand the difference between the Trade Execution
      Center and the Execution & Lifecycle Manager?
- [ ] Were you ever unsure whether an action you took was a real order or
      a simulation?

**General**
- [ ] At every page you visited, did you know what to do next?
- [ ] Did you ever feel lost — i.e., unsure how you got somewhere or how
      to get back?
- [ ] Did any page feel unfinished or broken (even if you understand now
      that it wasn't)?
- [ ] Would you know how to explain this platform to a colleague after
      today's session?

## Running the sessions

- Recruit testers who match the platform's real intended audience
  (people who already trade or invest, not general consumers) — the
  original review already established this product rewards patience and
  domain familiarity; testing it on someone with zero trading background
  would produce noise, not signal.
- Watch, don't narrate. Do not explain what a page does before the
  tester tries it — that defeats the entire point of a first-time-user
  test.
- Capture the free-form Journey walkthrough (Journeys 1–8) *before*
  handing over the Beta Checklist, and the Checklist *before* the
  Feedback Questions (`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Feedback-Questions.md`) — each
  successive document should prompt more structured reflection than the
  last, not replace the earlier, more honest first impression.
