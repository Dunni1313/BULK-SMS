# Version 1.6 — Beta Success Metrics

**Type:** Documentation and product-planning exercise. No application code
was written or modified to produce this document. Written directly on the
already-checked-out `v1.6.0-ux-polish-phase-1` branch.

**Purpose:** define, in advance, what "the beta succeeded" and "the beta
found a real problem" actually mean in measurable terms — before any
tester session happens, not retrofitted afterward to match whatever
results come in. Grounded in the same journeys, checklist, and questions
already built in `docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Testing-Guide.md` and
`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Feedback-Questions.md`, not a separate measurement
framework invented on top of them.

**Guiding principle:** these metrics measure **usability**, not feature
completeness or feature requests, per the explicit instruction governing
this entire phase. A beta that produces a long list of "I wish it also
did X" alongside strong usability numbers is a **successful** beta —
feature requests are an input to future planning (see
`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Operations-Guide.md` §B.4), not a beta failure signal.

---

## 1. Core Usability Metrics

Each metric below maps directly to one or more of the 8 journeys in
`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Testing-Guide.md` and is measured the same way for every
tester, so results are comparable across the cohort.

### 1.1 First-workflow completion rate

**Definition:** the tester completes Journey 1 (First Login) through
Journey 4 (First Trade Plan) — i.e., a full loop from a cold start to a
real, saved artifact (a trade plan) — without needing the facilitator to
intervene or explain anything not already visible on screen.

**How measured:** binary, per tester, recorded by the facilitator during
Session 1 (`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Operations-Guide.md` §A.3).

**Target:** **at least 6 of 8 testers (≥75%)** complete this loop
unassisted. Chosen deliberately below 100% — Journey 4 is explicitly
flagged in the Testing Guide as "the single highest-risk journey in the
whole beta" (the two non-interoperating Trade Plan systems), so some
friction here is expected and is itself a legitimate, useful finding, not
a target the product needs to hit perfectly to call the beta a success.

### 1.2 Wayfinding — "did the user know where to go next"

**Definition:** at each of the 8 journeys' own transition points (e.g.
"from Decision Workflow's own journey breadcrumb, does the tester
correctly find their way to Execution"), the tester proceeds via an
intended path (sidebar, journey breadcrumb, an in-page link, or the AI
Trading Coach's "Next Recommended Step") rather than trial-and-error
clicking, backtracking, or asking the facilitator.

**How measured:** the facilitator's own real-time note per transition,
during both facilitated sessions — a simple yes/no/"got there but with
visible hesitation" per transition, aggregated across all testers and all
transitions observed.

**Target:** **at least 70% of observed transitions** are a clean,
intended-path navigation. This is deliberately the metric most likely to
surface the sidebar naming-density and cross-linking gaps already
disclosed in `docs/v1.6.0-Known-Limitations.md` — a lower-than-target
score here is expected to correlate with, not contradict, that document's
own disclosures, and should be read as **validating** those known gaps
with real evidence, not as a surprise failure.

### 1.3 Research-to-decision completion

**Definition:** the tester completes Journey 3 (First Research Session)
and can, per that journey's own success criteria, "identify at least one
concrete signal the page is showing them, in their own words" — i.e., the
research surface (Trading Research, Value Research, or the Research
Terminal) genuinely informed them, not just rendered.

**How measured:** the facilitator's own judgment call during Session 1,
against the journey's own stated success criteria.

**Target:** **at least 7 of 8 testers (≥85%)**. This is one of the
platform's genuinely strong, previously-reviewed areas (multiple engines'
research surfaces were reviewed favorably in the original Product
Experience Review), so this target is intentionally higher than §1.1/§1.2.

### 1.4 Trade plan creation, correctly attributed

**Definition:** the tester not only creates a trade plan (§1.1) but, per
Journey 4's own success criteria, correctly predicts which other page(s)
will and won't later show that same plan — the actual test of whether the
Trade Plan disambiguation notes added in UX Polish Phase 1 genuinely
resolve the confusion they were built for, not just whether a plan gets
created at all.

**How measured:** a direct question the facilitator asks at the end of
Journey 4, per the Testing Guide's own explicit instruction not to skip
this step.

**Target:** **at least 5 of 8 testers (≥60%)** answer correctly. This is
deliberately the single lowest target in this document — the disambiguation
notes are a first attempt at a real architectural ambiguity, not a
guaranteed fix, and this metric exists specifically to measure whether
that first attempt worked, honestly, rather than to be hit.

### 1.5 Journal entry completion

**Definition:** the tester completes Journey 7 (First Journal Entry) —
writes and saves one real reflection — without needing to be told which
of the three "Journal"-named pages to use.

**How measured:** binary, per tester, facilitator-recorded during Session
2.

**Target:** **at least 6 of 8 testers (≥75%)**. Trading Journal
specifically was the single worst page in the platform for this exact
class of problem before UX Polish Phase 1 (the AI Trading Coach panel
previously occupying the entire visible screen) — this target is a direct
test of whether that fix generalizes to a genuinely first-time user, not
just to the person who made the fix.

### 1.6 Portfolio review comprehension

**Definition:** per Journey 8's own success criteria, the tester states
their account's overall health/risk read in their own words within 60
seconds of landing on the page.

**How measured:** timed, facilitator-recorded during Session 2.

**Target:** **at least 7 of 8 testers (≥85%)**, within 60 seconds. This
page family was independently flagged "already excellent" in the original
review — this target exists to confirm that assessment holds under real,
first-time-user conditions, not to introduce a new bar.

## 2. Negative / Warning Signal Metrics

These measure the *opposite* outcomes — signals that something is wrong,
tracked explicitly rather than only inferred from low scores above.

### 2.1 Required external help

**Definition:** any moment during a facilitated session where the tester
explicitly asks the facilitator "what do I click" / "where do I go" / "is
this working" and the facilitator has to answer beyond a neutral
acknowledgment.

**How measured:** a simple per-session count, facilitator-logged, tagged
to the specific journey/page it happened on.

**Threshold for concern:** **more than 2 such moments in a single
tester's combined Session 1 + 2** is a warning sign for that specific
journey — cross-reference against Part B of
`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Operations-Guide.md` to see if the same journey produced
a similar signal from other testers before treating a single
instance as significant.

### 2.2 Became genuinely stuck

**Definition:** a moment where the tester stops, cannot identify any next
action on their own, and would not have proceeded without facilitator
intervention (a stronger bar than §2.1 — not "asked for help" but
"reached an actual dead end").

**How measured:** binary per journey per tester, facilitator-logged.

**Threshold for concern:** **any single journey producing a "stuck"
moment for 2 or more of the 8 testers independently** — this is the
strongest, most objective signal in this entire document that a specific
page or flow needs attention before a wider release, since it means the
platform's own design, not one tester's individual unfamiliarity, is the
cause.

### 2.3 Confidence rating

**Definition:** directly reuses Feedback Question #15
(`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Feedback-Questions.md`: "After using the platform for
this session, would you trust it enough to use daily for real research —
even in Paper Trading mode? Why or why not?").

**How measured:** the tester's own free-text answer, read qualitatively
by the operator (not converted into a numeric score — the "why or why
not" is the actually useful part, and forcing it into a 1–10 number would
discard exactly the information this document's own guiding principle
cares about).

**Threshold for concern:** any answer that expresses genuine distrust of
whether an action was real vs. simulated (distinct from a simple
preference or feature gap) is treated as equivalent in severity to a
Critical finding for triage purposes — trust in the SIMULATED/Paper
Trading distinction is the single foundational property of this entire
platform, per `docs/v1.6.0-Known-Limitations.md`'s own "what feedback is
most valuable" ranking (#4).

## 3. Overall Beta Success Definition

**This beta is a success if:**

- Every §1 target above is met or exceeded **on average across the
  cohort** — individual testers falling short of one or two targets is
  expected and not itself a failure signal; a systematic, cohort-wide
  miss on a target is.
- No §2.2 "stuck" pattern (2+ independent testers, same journey) is found
  on more than 2 of the 8 journeys.
- No §2.3 genuine simulated-vs-real trust concern is raised by any tester.
- The feedback collected (per `docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Operations-Guide.md` Part
  B) is specific and actionable enough to inform a real prioritization
  decision afterward — i.e., the process itself produced usable signal,
  independent of whether the numbers above look good or bad.

**This does not mean a "perfect" beta** — per `docs/v1.6.0-Known-Limitations.md`,
several real, already-disclosed gaps (sidebar naming density, the two
Trade Plan systems, older un-migrated pages) are expected to be
re-confirmed, not discovered fresh, by real testers. Re-confirmation with
real evidence is itself a valuable, intended outcome of this beta, not a
sign it went poorly.

**This beta indicates a genuine need for another dedicated UX pass before
any wider release if:**

- Any §1 target misses by a wide margin (more than 20 percentage points
  below target) on a cohort-wide basis.
- A §2.2 "stuck" pattern appears on 3 or more of the 8 journeys.
- More than one tester raises a genuine §2.3 trust concern.
- The Beta Feedback Questions (`docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Feedback-Questions.md`)
  surface a *new* class of confusion not already named in
  `docs/v1.6.0-Known-Limitations.md` or the original Product Experience
  Review, affecting more than one tester.
