## 1. Objective

Build the next stage of the Platform Academy: 4 comprehensive Learning
Centre lessons covering the day-to-day trading workflow inside Engine 2
(the Institutional Trading Engine) and its cross-cutting Strategy Builder —
Market Structure Workbench, Trade Execution Centre, Strategy Builder
(`StrategyFramework.tsx` + `StrategyWorkbench.tsx`), and Trading Journal
(`TradingJournal.tsx`, Engine 2's own journal, distinct from Engine 3's
options-side journal). Every lesson follows the same rich-content shape
established in Sprints L1/L2A/L2B (Overview/Purpose, Screen Walkthrough
with every panel explained, step-by-step Workflow, Worked Examples, Common
Mistakes, Risk Warnings, Best Practices, AI Coach prompts, Related Modules,
Glossary integration, Bookmark support, Progress tracking, Knowledge Check
reusing the exact component L2B extracted) — no new Learning Centre
infrastructure was needed this sprint.

## 3. Design decisions made during implementation

- **Upgrade-in-place vs. new topic, per the L2A-established heuristic.**
  3 of the 4 requested modules already existed as plain, un-deepened
  topics whose keys directly matched the requested module — these were
  **upgraded in place** (key preserved, content replaced) to avoid
  duplicate lessons covering the same ground:
  - `trading-market-structure` (in the `trading-engine` path) →
    "Market Structure Workbench."
  - `strategy-framework-overview` (in the `strategy-framework` path) →
    "Strategy Builder: Create, Manage & Apply Your Own Strategies,"
    covering both `StrategyFramework.tsx` and `StrategyWorkbench.tsx`.
  - `trading-journal-review` (in the `trading-engine` path) →
    "Trading Journal: Recording, Reviewing & Reflecting on Your Trades."
  - The Trade Execution Centre required a genuinely **new** topic,
    `trade-execution-order-management`, since L2A's existing
    `options-income-engine-overview` already tours the Trade Execution
    Center at a summary/8-step level as part of that engine's own
    overview — duplicating that tour would have violated the explicit
    "do not duplicate code" requirement. The new topic is instead an
    explicitly disclosed **deeper companion** (its own header comment in
    `learningPaths.ts` states this directly), focusing specifically on
    the order ticket's own mechanics, position sizing, risk math, and
    closing a position — not restating the 8-step platform tour.
  - Placement: `trade-execution-order-management` was inserted into the
    `options-income-engine` path, immediately after `portfolio-ai-overview`
    (whose own `nextStepKeys` was updated to point to it), since
    `LessonRenderer.tsx`'s "Next Lesson" resolution remains intra-path
    only — the same constraint discovered in L2A and reaffirmed in L2B.
- **Honest disclosure over invented functionality, per the explicit
  "Use the actual implementation only. Do not invent functionality"
  requirement.** Three requested teaching points do not correspond to
  real, separate platform features:
  - "Stop Loss" / "Take Profit" (Trade Execution Centre) — reframed
    honestly as the structure's own Max Loss/Max Profit boundaries,
    fixed at entry by the chosen strikes, with closing handled via
    Roll/Convert or Position Management rather than a resting order.
  - "Running strategies" (Strategy Builder) — reframed honestly as
    manually applying a strategy's own Checklist Engine to a real
    symbol; there is no "Run Strategy" automation or backtest button.
  - "AI optimisation" (Strategy Builder) — reframed honestly as the
    deterministic Strategy Coach, which explains concepts and answers
    questions but never tunes a strategy's parameters or judges its
    methodology.
  In every case the lesson states the real, honest platform behavior
  plainly and explicitly discloses the gap between the requested framing
  and what the platform actually does, rather than silently omitting the
  topic or fabricating a feature that doesn't exist. The Strategy Builder
  lesson additionally carries a dedicated body paragraph naming both
  deliberate omissions directly, and one of its 3 Worked Examples is a
  "Poor Opportunity" scenario built specifically around a user
  incorrectly expecting automated execution/optimisation.
- **Cross-referencing instead of duplicating, for Trading Journal's
  Statistics/Win-Loss/AI-review requests.** Rather than inventing
  analytics that don't exist on `TradingJournal.tsx` itself, the lesson
  explicitly cross-references the real location of that functionality —
  the Trading AI Coach's own Journal and Psychology & Discipline coaches
  — and frames "daily review process" as a workflow composed from the
  journal entry itself plus those two coaches, never as a single
  dedicated feature.
- **No new Learning Centre infrastructure.** Sprint L2C introduces zero
  new schema fields, zero new components, and zero new progress-tracking
  item types — every one of the 4 lessons populates the exact
  `LearningTopic` shape (including `knowledgeCheck`) that already existed
  going into this sprint from L2A/L2B.
