# v1.6.0 — Product Experience Review

**Type:** Review only. No code was written, no branch was created, no files
in the application were modified, and no Pull Request was opened for this
document, per explicit instruction. This document was authored directly on
the already-checked-out `v1.6.0-sprint-03-ux-transformation` branch and is
**intentionally left uncommitted** — nothing here has been added to git.
The only exception to "do not modify the repository" is the creation of
this one file, since the task explicitly instructed "Create:
docs/v1.6.0-Product-Experience-Review.md." No other file was touched.

**Reviewer stance:** Product Designer / UX Director / Professional Trader /
First-time User / Product Owner — reviewed with no assumed prior knowledge
of the platform's own architecture, sprint history, or internal
terminology.

**Methodology:** This is not a source-code read-through. A real instance of
the application was built and run locally (real Postgres, real Express
backend, real Vite-served frontend), a brand-new user account was signed up
from scratch, and the platform was walked live in a real browser
(Playwright, 1440×900) starting at the Command Centre, capturing full-page
screenshots at each stop. 16 screens were directly observed this way:
`/login` (pre- and mid-signup), the Command Centre landing page, and 14
further pages spanning the golden path and a representative cross-section
of every major nav group (Scanner, Opportunity Pipeline, Trading Research,
Decision Workflow, Execution & Lifecycle Manager, Trade Execution Center,
Portfolio Dashboard, Trading Journal, Daily Report, Institutional Mentor,
Trade Planning & Risk Studio, Options Dashboard, Portfolio AI, Settings).
The sidebar's full navigation index (`nav-items.ts`, 93 items across 10
groups) was also read directly to ground the Navigation Review in the
platform's actual, complete route inventory rather than only the 16 pages
visited live.

**Disclosed scope boundary:** with 93 distinct nav items, a live
click-through of literally every page was not performed. The 16 pages
directly observed were chosen to cover the stated first-time-user
golden path (Discover → Research → Trade Plan → Risk Review → Execution →
Portfolio → Journal → Daily Review) plus at least one representative page
from every remaining nav group. The Page Review section below scores only
those 16 pages, each with a fully observed, evidence-backed justification —
no score is given for a page that was not actually opened. This mirrors
this project's own established practice of disclosing scope boundaries
honestly rather than fabricating coverage.

## Executive Summary

DK Option Engine has grown, sprint by sprint, into an extraordinarily deep
platform — three engines (Options Income, Institutional Trading,
Institutional Investing) plus a wide layer of cross-engine intelligence,
reporting, coaching, and learning modules, almost all of it built on a
genuine composition-over-duplication discipline (real reused calculations,
honest "insufficient data" states, consistent SIMULATED/Paper Trading
disclosure). Individually, several pages are genuinely excellent —
Institutional Mentor, Portfolio Dashboard, and Decision Workflow would not
look out of place in a real institutional terminal.

But reviewed as a first-time user actually trying to get through a normal
trading day, the platform today reads as **many well-built products
stitched together, not yet one product.** Four issues recur often enough
to dominate the first-time experience:

1. **The AI Trading Coach panel frequently eats the entire above-the-fold
   viewport.** On Scanner, Opportunity Pipeline, Trading Journal, Daily
   Report, Execution & Lifecycle Manager, and Trade Execution Center, a
   user lands on the page and sees only a chat-style coach card — no page
   title, no explanation of what the page is, no journey indicator — until
   they scroll. On Trading Journal and Daily Report this is total: nothing
   of the actual page is visible without scrolling at all.
2. **Naming duplication across the 93-item sidebar is severe.** There are
   4 pages with "Decision" in the name, 6 with "Institutional" in the
   Institutional Investing group alone, 5 separate AI chat/coach entry
   points, 3 "Journal" pages, and 3 "Backtest" pages. A first-time user has
   no way to know which of several similarly-named pages is the "right"
   one for a given task.
3. **Design language is inconsistent across page "eras."** Pages migrated
   to the new `PageShell` component (Scanner, Decision Workflow, Execution
   & Lifecycle Manager, Institutional Mentor, Opportunity Pipeline) have a
   consistent title/journey/next-action header. Older pages (Settings,
   Portfolio AI, Options Dashboard, Trading Research) do not, and sit right
   next to the new pages in the same sidebar with no visual signal that
   they're a different generation.
4. **A few small but real, trust-affecting bugs surfaced during a genuinely
   ordinary walkthrough** — most notably, Trading Research's sidebar
   footer reads "Sign in" while the user is demonstrably authenticated
   (personalized data is rendering on the page). A first-time user could
   reasonably conclude they've been logged out.

None of this reflects a lack of capability — the opposite is true. It
reflects a platform whose functional surface has grown faster than its
information architecture and visual consistency have kept up. The fixes
below are almost entirely navigation, layout, and wording changes, not new
features.

## First-Time User Review

For each page: did I understand what this page is? Did I understand why I
was here? Did I know what to do next? Was anything confusing? Did I have
to think about navigation? Did I ever feel lost?

### `/login`
Understood immediately — a clean, standard sign-in/sign-up form. **Issue:**
the full 93-item sidebar (10 groups) renders behind/beside the login form
even though there is no session yet, along with a notification bell
showing a stale badge count. A first-time visitor sees the entire product's
internal navigation before they've even created an account — confusing,
and it makes the product look like it forgot to gate a logged-out state.
Sign-up itself (name/email/password, "Sign up" button) was fast and
required no thought.

### Command Centre (`/`, post-signup)
Understood it was "home" quickly — bold "DK Institutional Command Centre"
framing invites confidence. Did I know what to do next? Mostly yes: Guided
Tours are front and center and clearly labelled. **Issues:** (a) I
immediately owned "3 open positions" and a "Portfolio Health Score" of 55
("Caution") despite having just created my account seconds earlier — I had
to consciously remind myself this must be seeded demo/paper data, because
nothing on screen told me so; (b) it being a Saturday/market-closed moment
in my walkthrough, most of the 11-step Daily Workflow displayed as greyed
"Not applicable today" or "Waiting for X" — the workflow read as mostly
broken/disabled rather than "come back during market hours."

### Scanner (`/scanner`)
**Did I know what this page was? Not right away.** The entire visible
screen on load is the "AI Trading Coach" welcome card — no page title, no
description, nothing scanner-specific — until I scrolled. Once scrolled,
the page itself (title, description, journey breadcrumb, "Next: Research")
is genuinely clear and well-organized. Also noticed "Market Scan" already
shows a green checkmark and 9% progress without my having clicked
anything — mildly confusing ("did something already run on my behalf?").
"Execution [Blocked]" shows with no inline reason.

### Opportunity Pipeline (`/opportunity-pipeline`)
Same coach-panel-dominates-the-fold issue as Scanner. Once scrolled past,
reasonably clear purpose.

### Trading Research (`/trading-research`)
Content itself (Market Structure card, real AAPL-style data) was clear and
useful. **Confusing/concerning:** the sidebar footer said "Sign in" even
though I was clearly logged in (personalized data was rendering). This
made me stop and wonder if my session had silently expired.

### Decision Workflow (`/decision-workflow`)
One of the clearest pages reviewed. Title, one-sentence purpose, a full
horizontal journey breadcrumb (Discover→...→Continuous Learning) with my
current stage highlighted, and an explicit "Next: Open Position" link. The
empty state ("No trade plans yet... create one first") was honest, but
**it pointed me to "Open the AI Assistant to create a Trade Plan"** — I
did not expect a chat assistant to be the way to create a formal Trade
Plan, especially since a dedicated "Trade Planning & Risk Studio" page
exists elsewhere in a completely different nav group with its own "Save
Trade Plan" button.

### Execution & Lifecycle Manager (`/execution-lifecycle`)
Same coach-panel-dominates issue. Once scrolled: clear enough, but I
immediately wondered how this related to "Trade Execution Center," a
differently-named page in a different nav group that visually looks almost
identical (same coach panel, same 11-step list, same "Execution [Blocked]"
badge).

### Trade Execution Center (`/trade-execution-center`)
Near-identical presentation to Execution & Lifecycle Manager. I could not,
as a first-time user, articulate what the difference between these two
pages is supposed to be from the page itself.

### Portfolio Dashboard (`/portfolio-dashboard`)
Excellent. Immediately understood this is a risk/health rollup. "Paper
Trading Mode," "Read-Only Portfolio Dashboard," and a "Learn" button are
all present up front — I trusted this page's numbers more than most others
I visited, specifically because it told me what it was and wasn't before I
had to ask.

### Trading Journal (`/trading-journal`)
**The single worst first-impression page reviewed.** Nothing but the AI
Trading Coach card is visible on load — no page title, no indication this
is even the Journal. I had to scroll to discover any journal content at
all existed.

### Daily Report (`/daily-report`)
Identical problem to Trading Journal — 100% coach panel above the fold.

### Institutional Mentor (`/institutional-mentor`)
Excellent. Title, purpose, four honest mode badges ("Institutional
Mentor," "Professional Portfolio Review," "Deterministic Analysis,"
"Paper Trading," "Educational Only"), a full journey breadcrumb, and a
9-category scorecard where every single score states, in plain sight, the
real number it was derived from. I trusted this page more than almost any
other. **One wording concern:** several scores cite their literal source
file/function (e.g. "portfolioConcentration.ts — breakdowns.strategy
.concentrationScore") — reassuring to an engineer, but reads like leaked
debug text to an actual end user.

### Trade Planning & Risk Studio (`/trade-planning-studio`)
Clear layout (Market Structure / Liquidity / Session / Trade Checklist on
the left, a step ribbon in the middle, Evidence/Notes/Journal/Coach on the
right). "Select an instrument to begin" is a clear call to action.
**Confusing:** several panels said "Insufficient data... No account value
provided" with no link telling me where to go set one.

### Options Dashboard (`/options-dashboard`)
A classic, dense trading dashboard — Account Value/Day P&L/Buying
Power/Open Positions tiles, Today's Top Pick, a symbol heat map, a Risk
Limit Breached banner. Understood it immediately as an experienced trader
would recognize this pattern from other platforms. As a genuine first-time
user, though, it's the densest, least-explained page of the walkthrough —
no "why am I here," no journey context, no PageShell treatment at all.

### Portfolio AI (`/portfolio-ai`)
Clear "Ravish AI cockpit" framing and a "SIMULATED" badge up front. Good
content (Portfolio Health, Market Exposure, Risk Concentration gauges).
No journey breadcrumb, no "why it matters," no PageShell — feels like an
older page sitting in a newer platform.

### Settings (`/settings`)
Plain, functional, and honestly the least "institutional-feeling" page of
the 16 reviewed — a bare "System Settings" heading with no badges, no
context, no journey nav, no explanation of what changing Execution Mode
actually does downstream. Understood the individual fields but not their
consequences.

## Navigation Review

The sidebar (`nav-items.ts`) contains **93 items across 10 groups**, plus a
"Frequently Used" pinned shortcut list and a global ⌘K command palette
(a genuine strength — it will find any item regardless of which group is
collapsed).

**Home** (13 items: Command Centre, Decision Workflow, Execution &
Lifecycle Manager, Portfolio & Risk Intelligence, Workflow Automation
Engine, Knowledge & Intelligence Graph, Playbooks & Operating Procedures,
Decision Quality & Review, Market Intelligence, Opportunity Pipeline,
Personal Dashboard, Options Command Center, Notifications, Operations
Dashboard). *Does it belong here?* Only about 3 of these (Command Centre,
Notifications, Personal Dashboard) feel like "Home" in the ordinary sense;
the other ~9 are full, heavyweight cross-cutting engines that happen to
have been placed in "Home" because they don't belong to one specific
existing engine. *Would a beginner understand it?* No — a beginner opening
"Home" expecting a landing-page shortcut list would instead find 13
unrelated modules of wildly different weight. **Recommendation: split into
"Home" (genuinely just the 3 landing items) and a second, honestly-named
group for the cross-cutting engines** (e.g. "Cross-Engine Intelligence" or
similar) — see Navigation Improvement #1.

**Options Trading** (15 items). *Does it belong here?* Yes, this reads as
the original, coherent Options Income Engine flow. *Is anything
duplicated?* Yes — "Trade Execution Center" sits here while a very similar
"Execution & Lifecycle Manager" sits in Home; and this group's own
"Backtest"/"Leaderboard"/"Journal" trio (explicitly disclosed in the file's
own comments as legacy, kept for backward compatibility) sit alongside
newer, differently-named equivalents elsewhere ("Options Backtest" in this
same group; "Trading Backtest" and "Trading Journal" in Trading Workbench).

**Options Income Engine** (10 items). Reads as a coherent, if densely
named, "Institutional X Engine" set. *Could it be renamed?* The group name
and its own sibling "Options Trading" group name are close enough
("Options Trading" vs. "Options Income Engine") that a beginner may not
immediately grasp these are two different groups rather than one split
awkwardly in half.

**Portfolio Management** (12 items: Portfolio AI, Portfolio Dashboard,
Institutional Portfolio Workspace, Institutional Workspace, AI Portfolio
Analyst, Stress Test, Event Risk, Concentration Risk, Performance, Trade
History, Trade Performance, Paper Portfolio). *Is anything duplicated?*
"Institutional Portfolio Workspace" and "Institutional Workspace" are one
word apart and both about portfolio work — genuinely hard to tell apart by
name alone. "Portfolio AI" and "AI Portfolio Analyst" are also easy to
confuse (same two words, reversed).

**Institutional Investing** (12 items) sits right next to **Value
Investing** (5 items) — both are, in effect, "research and evaluate a
company" groups. *Would a beginner understand the difference?* Unlikely.
Within Institutional Investing alone, six items start with "Institutional"
(Dashboard, Intelligence, Mentor, Monitoring, Reporting Centre, AI Coach)
with no other distinguishing word in most of their names — this is the
single densest naming-collision zone in the whole sidebar.

**Trading Workbench** (11 items) is internally coherent (real
technical-analysis tooling: Market Structure, Liquidity, Trade Planning,
Strategy Framework), but its own "Trading Journal," "Trading Backtest,"
and "Trading AI Coach" each have a same-concept sibling elsewhere in the
sidebar under a shorter name (Journal, Backtest, and 4 other AI-named
entry points respectively).

**AI & Decision Tools** (4 items: AI Assistant, AI Trading Assistant, AI
Trade Journal, Cross-Engine Workspace) is small and, ironically, does not
contain most of the platform's AI-named pages — those are scattered across
Home, Institutional Investing, and Trading Workbench. The grouping
principle behind what does vs. doesn't belong here isn't visible from the
sidebar itself.

**Learning Centre** (9 items) — coherent and easy to understand; no
concerns.

**Administration** (2 items: AutoPilot, Settings) — a slightly unusual
pairing (a live kill-switch control next to app configuration), but small
enough not to be confusing.

**General navigation recommendations** (no implementation, per
instruction):
- Split "Home" per above.
- Establish one consistent noun for every AI chat/coach surface ("Coach"
  or "Assistant," not both) and consider whether 5 separate entry points
  are genuinely necessary or could be one assistant with contextual modes.
- Cross-link or visually distinguish same-concept siblings (the 4
  "Decision" pages, 3 "Journal" pages, 3 "Backtest" pages, "Institutional
  Portfolio Workspace" vs. "Institutional Workspace").
- Consider merging "Institutional Investing" and "Value Investing" into
  one group, or clearly subdividing them with a one-line description of
  what belongs in each.
- Several long group labels visibly truncate in the sidebar itself
  ("Execution & Lifecycl...", "Portfolio & Risk Intell...", "Knowledge &
  Intellig...", "Decision Quality & R...", "Options Command C...",
  "Institutional Investi..."); confirm every one has a hover tooltip
  showing the full name.

## Command Centre Review

**Is this genuinely the Home page?** Structurally, yes — it is the app's
literal `/` route and the place a fresh sign-up lands. **Does it naturally
become the place users return to?** Plausibly, given its breadth (account
snapshot, top pick, symbol heat map, risk banner, theta income, Guided
Tours), but its sheer information density works against the "quick daily
check-in" framing a Command Centre implies.

**Does it clearly explain today's priorities?** Partially. "Today's Top
Pick" and the Daily Workflow progress list are genuine priority signals.
But with 8 of 11 workflow steps reading "Not applicable" or "Waiting for X"
outside market hours, and a Risk Limit Breached banner appearing
immediately for a brand-new seeded account, the page's loudest signals on
a first visit are two things a new user did nothing to cause and cannot
yet interpret.

**Does it reduce decision fatigue?** Not yet, in its current form. A page
whose job is to reduce decision fatigue should surface one clear "do this
next" — the Daily Workflow's own "Next Recommended Step" card does exactly
this and is the strongest single element on the page — but it currently
competes for attention with a dozen other tiles/panels of comparable visual
weight. Promoting that one "Next Recommended Step" card higher and larger,
and quieting everything else until it's asked for, would make this
Command Centre's job much clearer.

## Workflow Review

Walking Discover → Research → Trade Plan → Risk Review → Execution →
Portfolio → Journal → Daily Review as a continuous day:

- **Discover** (Scanner) → **Research** (Trading Research): the
  PlatformJourneyNav's own "Next: Research" link works correctly and is a
  genuine strength — no dead end, no extra click. *Friction:* Scanner's own
  identity is hidden below the coach panel on arrival.
- **Research** → **Trade Plan**: no direct link was found from Trading
  Research to a trade-planning page; the user is expected to navigate via
  the sidebar. *Friction:* once at Trade Plan territory, there are **two**
  places that talk about creating a Trade Plan — Decision Workflow's own
  "Open the AI Assistant to create a Trade Plan" link, and the dedicated
  Trade Planning & Risk Studio's own "Save Trade Plan" button — with no
  cross-link between them. **This is a genuine duplicate-action risk**: a
  first-time user could create a plan one way, then later look in the
  other place and not find it, or wonder which is authoritative.
- **Trade Plan** → **Risk Review**: Trade Planning & Risk Studio does
  contain risk panels (Portfolio Risk Budget, Position Size Review)
  directly alongside planning — a genuine workflow win, one page instead
  of two. *Friction:* "Insufficient data" messages here have no inline
  path to resolve them.
- **Risk Review** → **Execution**: this is where naming duplication does
  the most damage. Two pages — Execution & Lifecycle Manager and Trade
  Execution Center — present near-identical layouts (same coach panel,
  same 11-step list, same "Execution [Blocked]" badge) in two different
  nav groups. A first-time user has no way to know which one is "the"
  execution page for their plan.
- **Execution** → **Portfolio**: clean; Portfolio Dashboard is one of the
  strongest pages in the platform and clearly closes the loop from "I
  executed something" to "here's my risk/health now."
- **Portfolio** → **Journal**: functionally fine (sidebar link works), but
  Trading Journal's own landing state (100% coach panel, zero visible
  journal content) is a genuine dead end on first impression — a user
  could reasonably bounce off this page believing it's broken or empty
  before ever scrolling.
- **Journal** → **Daily Review**: same issue repeats on Daily Report.

**Extra clicks:** scrolling past a full-height coach panel functions as an
extra "click" (scroll) on 6 of the 16 pages reviewed before any real
content is visible.
**Dead ends:** none were absolute (every page did eventually offer a next
step once scrolled/explored), but Trading Journal and Daily Report come
close on first impression.
**Confusing wording:** "Execution [Blocked]" with no inline reason; "No
trade plans yet... Open the AI Assistant to create a Trade Plan" pointing
away from the dedicated planning page.
**Duplicate actions:** trade-plan creation (AI Assistant vs. Trade
Planning & Risk Studio); execution tracking (Execution & Lifecycle Manager
vs. Trade Execution Center).
**Navigation friction:** moving between Research, Trade Plan, and
Execution requires knowing which of several similarly-named sidebar items
to pick — the golden path is not fully continuous via in-page links the
way Discover→Research is.

## Product Review

**Would I enjoy using this every day?** As a professional trader, parts of
it — yes, genuinely. Portfolio Dashboard, Institutional Mentor, and
Decision Workflow are the kind of pages I'd open daily without hesitation;
they're honest, well-organized, and clearly labelled. But the recurring
coach-panel-hides-the-page issue and the sheer number of similarly-named
pages would cause real daily friction — I'd likely bookmark 4–5 specific
pages and avoid navigating the sidebar at all, which undercuts the "one
unified platform" ambition.

**Would I recommend it?** To a sophisticated, patient trader who wants
serious institutional-grade analytical depth and is willing to spend a few
sessions learning the platform's own map — yes. To a first-time trader
looking for something intuitive out of the box — not yet; the 93-item,
heavily-duplicated navigation and inconsistent page design language would
be a real barrier before they ever get to appreciate how much genuine
capability is underneath.

**Would I pay for it?** For the depth of analysis (real composed
Investment Committee verdicts, real risk decomposition, honest
SIMULATED/Paper Trading disclosure everywhere) — yes, this is worth
paying for as a research and risk tool. I would want the navigation and
consistency issues addressed first before recommending it be sold as a
polished, ready-to-use daily terminal, though — right now the perceived
value is diluted by how much effort it takes to find the right page.

**What still feels unfinished?**
- Visual/structural consistency between "PageShell-era" pages and older
  pages (Settings, Portfolio AI, Options Dashboard, Trading Research).
- The AI Trading Coach panel's placement relative to page identity.
- The sheer number of same-concept pages under different names, several of
  which look nearly identical once opened (Execution & Lifecycle Manager /
  Trade Execution Center being the clearest example).
- A first-time-user explanation of why a brand-new account already has
  seeded trades and a "Caution" risk score.

## Top 20 UX Improvements

1. Move or collapse the AI Trading Coach panel so page identity (title,
   description, journey stage) always renders above the fold, on every
   page — the single highest-impact fix identified in this review.
2. Fix the Trading Research sidebar footer showing "Sign in" while
   authenticated — a trust-eroding bug, not a cosmetic nitpick.
3. Consolidate Execution & Lifecycle Manager and Trade Execution Center
   into one page, or make their differing purpose obvious from within each
   page itself, not just by memorizing which sidebar group they're in.
4. Resolve the "where do I create a Trade Plan" ambiguity between Decision
   Workflow's "Open the AI Assistant" link and Trade Planning & Risk
   Studio's own "Save Trade Plan" button — cross-link them at minimum.
5. Add an inline reason next to "Execution [Blocked]" instead of requiring
   the user to find a separate "Market Closed" badge elsewhere on the page.
6. Don't silently auto-complete a workflow step (Scanner's "Market Scan")
   without user action, or explain clearly why it auto-completed.
7. Hide the full sidebar (or use a minimal layout) on the pre-authentication
   `/login` page; a stale notification badge should never be visible before
   sign-in.
8. Explain `ensureSeedTrades()`'s auto-seeded starter positions to a
   brand-new user instead of silently presenting "3 open positions" and a
   "Caution" health score with no context.
9. When markets are closed, reframe the Daily Workflow's empty states
   around what CAN be done right now, rather than a wall of "Not
   applicable today" / "Waiting for X" that reads as broken.
10. Migrate the remaining high-traffic older pages (Settings, Portfolio
    AI, Options Dashboard, Trading Journal, Daily Report) onto the same
    PageShell pattern used elsewhere.
11. Replace raw internal source references shown as end-user copy (e.g.
    "portfolioDashboard.ts — healthFactors.position_sizing_quality") with
    plain-English explanations; keep the technical trace as an optional,
    secondary disclosure for power users.
12. Give "insufficient data" states an inline, actionable next step (e.g.
    Trade Planning & Risk Studio's account-value gap) instead of stating
    the gap with nothing to do about it.
13. Standardize how "why this page exists" is surfaced — some pages use
    PageShell's collapsed panel, several older ones show nothing at all.
14. Reduce or clearly differentiate the 5 separate AI chat/coach entry
    points (AI Assistant, AI Trading Assistant, Trading AI Coach,
    Institutional AI Coach, plus the embedded AI Trading Coach panel
    itself) right where a user first encounters each one.
15. Make the AI Trading Coach panel collapsible-and-remembered across
    visits, not something to scroll past fresh on every page load.
16. On Command Centre, promote the single "Next Recommended Step" card
    above the dozen other tiles competing for attention, to genuinely
    reduce decision fatigue rather than just organize it.
17. Make the difference between the six "Institutional X" pages
    (Dashboard/Intelligence/Mentor/Monitoring/Reporting Centre/AI Coach)
    scannable at a glance — a one-line subtitle or hover tooltip per item.
18. Give brand-new users a one-time, skippable explainer for what a Paper
    Trading account is and why they already have starter trades.
19. Confirm every truncated sidebar group label has a hover tooltip
    showing its full name.
20. Unify the visual language of every "mode" badge across the platform
    (SIMULATED, Paper Trading Mode, Advisory Only, Educational Only,
    Read-Only, Deterministic Analysis) into one consistent badge style, so
    trust signals read the same everywhere.

## Top 10 Navigation Improvements

1. Split the 13-item "Home" group into a genuine "Home" (Command Centre,
   Notifications, Personal Dashboard) and a separately, honestly-named
   group for the 9 heavyweight cross-cutting engines currently hidden
   inside it.
2. Cross-link or visually distinguish the 4 "Decision"-named pages
   (Decision Workflow, Decision Engine, Decision Support Engine, Decision

## Overall Scores

- **Overall Product Score: 8/10.** The functional depth, composition
  discipline, and honesty of disclosure (SIMULATED/Paper Trading/
  Educational Only/insufficient-data states) across dozens of modules is
  genuinely impressive and rarely seen at this scope. The weakest link is
  not capability but cohesion.
- **Overall UX Score: 5.5/10.** Individually strong pages sit inside a
  navigation structure with severe naming duplication, and a component
  (the AI Trading Coach panel) that frequently obscures page identity on
  first load. Functional, but not yet intuitive for a genuine first-time
  user.
- **Overall Professional Quality Score: 7/10.** Several pages
  (Institutional Mentor, Portfolio Dashboard, Decision Workflow) look and
  feel like a real institutional terminal. The aggregate impression is
  diminished by visible inconsistency between page "eras" and by the two
  or three real, visible bugs found during an entirely ordinary
  walkthrough.

## Release Recommendation

**Not yet ready for a wide, first-time-user-facing General Availability
release in its current state.** The underlying product is strong enough to
justify continued investment, but the issues in this review are exactly
the kind that determine whether a new user's first session builds
confidence or erodes it — and several (the coach panel hiding page
identity on 6+ pages, the sign-in-state bug, the Execution page naming
collision) are concrete enough to fix without any new feature work.

**Recommended path:** run a dedicated consolidation sprint before wider
release, scoped narrowly to: (1) coach-panel placement relative to page
identity, (2) the Trading Research sign-in-state bug, (3) the
Execution-page naming/duplication pair, and (4) migrating the remaining
older pages onto the shared PageShell pattern. None of this requires new
analytical capability — it is entirely a navigation, layout, and wording
pass on top of what already exists. Release to existing power users /
beta testers who already understand the platform's map can reasonably
continue in the meantime.

**Per the task's explicit instruction: nothing in this review has been
implemented. This document is a review only, delivered for approval before
any further development.**
