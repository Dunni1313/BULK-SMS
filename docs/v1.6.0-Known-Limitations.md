# v1.6.0 — Known Limitations (Beta)

**Type:** Documentation and product-planning exercise only — no code
changes, per the same disclosure in `docs/v1.6.0-Beta-Testing-Guide.md`.

**Purpose:** tell beta testers, plainly, what is intentionally
unfinished, what is deferred, what to ignore, and what kind of feedback
is actually useful right now. A tester who reports something already
listed here isn't wasting anyone's time — but knowing it's already known
lets them spend their limited session time on things we don't yet know
about instead.

---

## What is intentionally unfinished

### Two, non-interoperating "Trade Plan" systems

The platform genuinely has two separate Trade Plan systems: one created
via the AI Assistant (used by Decision Workflow, Execution & Lifecycle
Manager, and Playbooks) and a second, symbol-specific one used by Trade
Planning & Risk Studio, Trade Workspace, and the Market Structure/
Liquidity Workbenches. As of v1.6.0 UX Polish Phase 1, every page that
could be mistaken for the other now says so explicitly and links across
— but the two systems themselves remain separate. **This is a known,
disclosed architectural decision, not a bug you need to report** unless
the disambiguation text itself is missing, wrong, or confusing on a page
you visit.

### Sidebar naming density

Several groups of pages share a first word with no further distinction
visible in the sidebar itself: 4 "Decision"-named pages, 3
"Journal"-named pages, 3 "Backtest"-named pages, 6 "Institutional
X"-named pages inside Institutional Investing, and 5 separate AI
chat/coach entry points. Every link now has a hover tooltip with its full
name, but the deeper rename/consolidation work is intentionally deferred
— it requires coordinated changes across both the sidebar and each page's
own title, which is a larger, higher-risk change than a beta-stage polish
pass should attempt.

### Older pages haven't been visually unified yet

Settings, Portfolio AI, and Options Dashboard predate the newer
`PageShell` header pattern (title + description + journey breadcrumb +
badges) used on Scanner, Decision Workflow, Execution & Lifecycle
Manager, and others. They work correctly, but look and feel like an
earlier generation of the product. This is cosmetic, known, and queued
for a future pass — please don't report "this page looks different" on
its own; do report it if the *inconsistency* itself caused real
confusion about what a page does.

### Seeded starter data on a brand-new account

Every new signup automatically owns 3 example trades and a computed
Portfolio Health Score, via `ensureSeedTrades()` — this is intentional,
existing behavior meant to give a new account something to look at
immediately, not a bug. Nothing on screen currently explains this to a
first-time user, which is the actual, still-open gap: not that the data
exists, but that its origin is unexplained.

### AI Trading Coach step list starts collapsed

As of UX Polish Phase 1, the coach's full 11-step checklist is collapsed
by default (behind "View all N steps"), so a page's own content is
visible immediately. If you expected to see the full checklist without
clicking, that's the intended new behavior, not a regression.

## What is deferred

- **A decision on whether the two Trade Plan systems should eventually be
  unified.** Explicitly flagged as a real, open architecture question in
  `docs/v1.6.0-UX-Polish-Phase-1.md` — not decided, not scheduled.
- **The full sidebar rename/consolidation pass** referenced above.
- **Migrating Settings, Portfolio AI, and Options Dashboard onto
  `PageShell`.**
- **Investigating the Command Centre's notification-bell count.** During
  live re-validation for UX Polish Phase 1, a brand-new account's
  notification badge showed a nonzero count ("165") with no
  corresponding notifications to explain it — flagged, not yet
  investigated.
- **A small residual authentication-timing nuance.** On a genuine
  full-page browser reload (not normal in-app navigation), the sidebar
  can very briefly show "Sign in" before settling on "Signed in as…" —
  traced to how the underlying auth library's own session-refresh logic
  can abort and retry its own request. The original, persistent version
  of this bug (an authenticated user's sidebar staying wrong
  indefinitely) is fixed and re-verified; this is a narrower, rarer,
  self-correcting residual, not a full regression.

## What users should ignore

- **"SIMULATED" and "Paper Trading" labels are not errors** — they are
  the platform's own honest disclosure that a given number or action
  isn't touching real money or a real brokerage account. This is by
  design across the entire platform and will not change during beta.
- **A page reading "Insufficient data"** (e.g., Trade Planning & Risk
  Studio's Portfolio Risk Budget without an account value entered) is the
  platform declining to fabricate a number it can't honestly compute —
  not a bug. If you hit one of these and can't figure out how to *supply*
  the missing input, that's worth reporting; the honest-refusal behavior
  itself is not.
- **Market-closed states.** Outside real market hours, several workflow
  steps and the Execution page will honestly show as blocked or not
  applicable. This is accurate, not broken — the underlying market really
  is closed.
- **Different visual styling between older and newer pages,** as
  described above — already known, already queued.

## What feedback is most valuable right now

In priority order, the feedback that will do the most good during this
beta:

1. **Any moment you felt lost or unsure what to do next**, especially if
   it happened on a page not already named in this document or in
   `docs/v1.6.0-UX-Polish-Phase-1.md`'s own "remaining issues" list — new
   discoveries are worth far more right now than re-confirmations of
   already-known gaps.
2. **Anywhere the disambiguation text added in UX Polish Phase 1** (the
   Trade Plan notes, the Execution Center vs. Lifecycle Manager notes)
   **didn't actually resolve your confusion**, even though it's present
   on screen — this is a genuine open question about whether the fix
   worked, not just whether it exists.
3. **Wording that reads as unclear, alarming, or overly technical** —
   e.g., anything that sounds like a raw error message, a developer
   term, or a number with no context for whether it's good or bad.
4. **Anything that made you doubt whether an action was real or
   simulated** — trust in that distinction is foundational to this
   product and worth flagging even for a small moment of doubt.
5. **Whether the platform's overall depth and seriousness came through**
   — i.e., did it feel like a genuine institutional-grade tool, or did
   any part of the experience undercut that impression. This is
   subjective, but it's exactly the kind of signal a structured QA pass
   can't surface on its own.

**What is explicitly not useful right now:** feature requests ("I wish it
also did X"), or reports that only restate an item already listed above
as intentionally deferred. Both are welcome to note informally, but they
won't shape this beta's own priorities.
